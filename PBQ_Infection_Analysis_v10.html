Exam & Real-World Strategy for Log Analysis:
  • Step 1: Find the Anomaly (The 'What'): Start with broad network logs (like a perimeter firewall). Look for an external IP address communicating with multiple internal hosts, especially if it exhibits regular, repeating connections. Rely on your knowledge of standard ports (e.g., DNS on 53, HTTPS on 443) to quickly filter out normal, expected traffic. Find the outlier.
  • Step 2: Trace the Origin (The 'Where' & 'When'): Once you identify a malicious indicator (the external IP), trace it backward chronologically. Which internal IP made the first allowed connection to it? That is your origin point. Correlate that timestamp with the host's local logs to discover the initial vector.
  • Step 3: Track Lateral Movement (The 'How'): After establishing a foothold, a threat will often attempt to spread. Look in the network logs for the origin host initiating internal-to-internal traffic. Pay close attention to common exploitation and administrative ports (like 445 for SMB, 3389 for RDP, or 22 for SSH).
  • Step 4: Distinguish "Targeted" vs "Infected" (The 'Who'): Just because an attack reached a PC does not mean the compromise was successful. Check the individual host logs for the outcome. Log entries like "QUARANTINED" or "Connection blocked" mean the host's security controls successfully defended it (the host is Clean). Entries like "Process started", services unexpectedly stopping, or unauthorized account creation indicate it is Infected.
